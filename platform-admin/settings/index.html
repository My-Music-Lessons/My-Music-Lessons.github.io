Nobody, ever 4PB-0015

  • What earns the upliftFour conditions, every session, 24 hours

    Clocking in on time, attendance for every student, practice notes, all of it inside twenty-four hours. One session short on any one and the month pays the base rate.

    What changing it does not do. There is no setting here at all, and that is the point: a condition that can be relaxed per teacher is not a condition, it is a favour.

    Reaches: Every month, every teacher, the same way. · Set in: Nowhere. It is a rule, and it changes by being ruled on.

    The rules behind it · 20
    1. PB-0045 Goodwill is a free online lesson where something went badly. It is not a refund, it changes no charge and no pay line, and nobody is owed it.
    2. PB-0058 Twenty-four hours is the window for the month's uplift. Miss it and the uplift goes; everything else can still be put right.
    3. PB-0060 One session short is enough to lose the month's uplift. The lessons are still paid at base rate and the families are still charged.
    4. PB-0062 What the teacher's phone recorded stays on the record. An adjustment sits beside it, never over it.
    5. PB-0063 At lock, buckets and lines become immutable. Nothing on a run can be edited afterwards by anybody for any reason.
    6. PB-0064 A locked figure is corrected only by a pay adjustment of the opposite sign that points at it. The original line is never touched.
    7. PB-0065 Blocked or held pay is delayed, never lost. It is listed on the invoice as pending so nobody has to remember it, and arrives as an adjustment next period.
    8. PB-0067 A bucket is one teacher at one pair of rates on one VAT treatment over one stretch of the month. Rounding happens once per bucket and the remainder goes on the last line, so the lines always add to the bucket.
    9. PB-0068 A pay line names the lesson, the school and the date. It never names a child.
    10. PB-0071 A closure left undecided holds the payroll lock. The run cannot close while somebody might still be owed for a visit.
    11. PB-0155 Practice note not written: to the teacher at 24 hours, once, because the uplift is already gone at that point.
    12. PB-0230 An online teaching hour is paid at £20.00. There is no uplift on it and there never was one to lose.
    13. PB-0231 An in-school teaching hour is paid at £20.00 base, and the OTE uplift applies to in-school hours only. The uplift is still monthly and still all-or-nothing on the four conditions.
    14. PB-0232 Wherever the teacher portal states a rate it states which kind of hour it is for. One blended figure across both is the line a teacher will quote back at us, and they will be right to.
    15. PB-0233 A make-up is online, so a teacher covering one earns base for those hours. The portal says so at the point the teacher takes the session, not afterwards on a statement.
    16. PB-0234 The browser never works out a rate, an uplift or a total. Every figure on a pay screen is supplied, including the split between online and in-school hours.
    17. PB-0235 Attendance on an online lesson is not something the teacher types. The session knows who signed in and who taught it, so the register is taken from that. A teacher marking a register for a lesson the system watched them deliver is asking them to confirm what we already know.
    18. PB-0236 Practice notes ARE still required on an online lesson. The parent reads them and the next teacher relies on them, and neither of those stops being true because the hour is paid at base.
    19. PB-0237 A missing online practice note is never marked against the in-school OTE. The uplift is decided by in-school sessions alone, and nothing that happens on an online lesson can take it away.
    20. PB-0238 An online practice note that has not been written is CHASED. Not penalised, chased: the teacher is reminded until it is in, because the thing we want is the note, and the one lever we have given up is the money one.
  • A teacher's noticeSet by the signed agreement, not by this screen

    How much notice a teacher gives, and we give, under the Teacher Services Agreement.

    What changing it does not do. Changing a number here would not change anybody's notice. It is a term of a signed contract, and it changes by a new version somebody signs.

    Reaches: Nothing until a new version is signed. · Set in: The agreement, and the signature record behind it.

    The rules behind it · 16
    1. PB-0017 Edit a signature, or the text it was signed against.
    2. PB-0029 Nothing is priced by inference. A line has the rate its agreement gives it on the date it applies, or it is an exception and is shown as one. No fallback rate, no average, no rounding to make a total tidy.
    3. PB-0088 The person enrolling has to be named on the school's agreement as able to commit the school, and has to be at that school.
    4. PB-0089 The agreement has to say the school may fund places, not only that it hosts lessons.
    5. PB-0093 Every signed record has exactly one signature event, and the hash of the text at signing equals the hash of the template it points at. Not roughly: exactly one, and equal.
    6. PB-0095 A template is rendered when it is offered, not when it is signed, and both hashes are kept.
    7. PB-0098 A stopped template is not replaced by removing it. A corrected version has to be written, approved and put in use, and until then anything gated on it cannot proceed.
    8. PB-0099 Signature events are append-only. A mistake is a new record, and both stay.
    9. PB-0116 A paid lesson cannot be deleted or quietly moved. It is covered, moved with the family's agreement, or credited back. There is no way to dismiss one.
    10. PB-0144 Nothing is sent that a real person would not have written - no template seams, no unresolved fields, no 'Dear Parent/Guardian'.
    11. PB-0156 Signature outstanding: at seven days and at fourteen, then it becomes a case rather than a reminder.
    12. PB-0171 Chase a missing register, a missing note, a missing signature, missing term dates.
    13. PB-0176 A closing funded place does not convert to the family. Converting would put a family into a consumer contract they never signed, so the place closes and the family is offered a new one on their own terms if they want it.
    14. PB-0181 School agreement v1.1 is approved for issue, and v1.0 is withdrawn. Clause 8.5 made an assurance about where data is held that could not be evidenced, and an assurance we cannot evidence does not go out under our name.
    15. PB-0185 The same answer applies the other way round. A school that ends with us does not keep the teachers we placed there and run the service itself. It happens, and the answer is the same no.
    16. PB-0186 Neither answer has any force until the wording exists in the contracts. The teacher agreement and the school agreement both need it, drafted by a solicitor, heavy rather than polite.

One name, audited 4PB-0002

  • The make-up window42 days, and no expiry at all where the cause was ours

    How long a family has to take a make-up before they are offered a credit or the week's rate back instead.

    What changing it does not do. Shortening it does not shorten a make-up already issued. Every make-up carries the window it was issued under, which is why this is the setting people get wrong.

    Reaches: Make-ups issued after the change. · Set in: The service policy.

    The rules behind it · 26
    1. PB-0001 The engine, alone. For things that must happen the same way every time, whoever is watching: converting a missed week to a make-up, extending a make-up window, blocking pay that has no evidence behind it. No person, and the record says which rule acted.
    2. PB-0019 Put an expiry on a make-up we caused.
    3. PB-0042 Missed lessons are made up. They are not refunded and they are not deducted.
    4. PB-0043 A make-up is a lesson, not money. It changes no charge.
    5. PB-0047 Billing continues through holidays and closures alike. A subscription is priced across the year, so a holiday week is already paid for in the weeks either side. The week converts to a make-up.
    6. PB-0048 A make-up runs 20 minutes, or 30 where the plan is the 30-minute one. It is the plan's make-up length, not the length of the lesson missed.
    7. PB-0049 A make-up expires two weeks after it is issued. Two weeks or nothing, and it is a fair offer rather than a mean one: a make-up is delivered ONE TO ONE while the lesson it replaces was usually a group, so the family gets something worth more than what they lost and it costs us several times the teacher time per child. Who caused the miss no longer changes the expiry - that used to mean a make-up we caused never expired, which was fair and was exactly what racked them up. The single exception is our own lack of availability, which holds the clock.
    8. PB-0050 The cause of a make-up is set when it is issued and is immutable afterwards, except by an admin with a reason and a second approver.
    9. PB-0054 A school-funded child and a family-paid child have the same entitlement, the same make-up and the same expiry rules. A change of funding never silently expires or duplicates a make-up.
    10. PB-0055 A pupil starting during a holiday week gets no make-up for that week. A start date is always the first real teaching date on the slot's weekday.
    11. PB-0059 Forty-eight hours with no attendance at all raises an exception. The cause is undetermined from then, so there is no pay and no make-up until it is captured.
    12. PB-0109 A proposal expires seven days after it is made. A stale proposal committed on day nine is worse than no proposal at all.
    13. PB-0126 A family on a school-funded place may be told their child's lesson time, that a make-up has been issued, and a change of teacher, room or timetable.
    14. PB-0152 Make-up issued: same day, with how to book it and when it runs out.
    15. PB-0153 Make-up expiring: fourteen days before and three days before, and never on the day itself.
    16. PB-0163 Convert a missed week to a make-up.
    17. PB-0164 Extend a make-up window where fewer than three slots remain.
    18. PB-0211 A parent who questions an online make-up is answered with what it is before it is answered with money. It is a live lesson with a real teacher, one to one, in the Online tab of their app; they pick a time from whoever is available and book it themselves; and it keeps the child playing in the week that would otherwise have been lost. No credit and no refund is mentioned in the first answer, because mentioning it is offering it.
    19. PB-0212 If the parent is still unhappy and the exchange has reached four or five messages, offer a credit against their next invoice. Not sooner. An offer made in the second message teaches a family that pressing produces money, and it takes a lesson away from a child who could have had it.
    20. PB-0213 If they are still unhappy after a further three or four messages, offer a refund. That is the end of the ladder. It does not overturn the rule that missed lessons are made up rather than refunded: the make-up is still the remedy, and this says when we offer, not who authorises. A refund reached this way is the discretionary kind and goes through approval like any other.
    21. PB-0214 Online make-ups are issued automatically on attendance. Nobody issues an additional one by hand, for any reason, including to settle an argument. The job is to manage what a child is already owed - find it, hold it, book it, extend it where the rules allow - never to create more.
    22. PB-0215 A credit and a refund each remove the online make-up they settle. They are alternatives to the lesson, never additions to it: a family takes the lesson or takes the money. An entitlement that has been paid out is gone the moment the money is agreed, not when somebody remembers.
    23. PB-0216 The amount is one quarter of that family's monthly subscription, and it is the same figure whether it goes back as cash or onto the next invoice. The engine supplies it. Nothing in a browser divides a subscription by four, for the same reason nothing in a browser adds up a bill.
    24. PB-0217 Where a quarter of a monthly subscription does not land on whole pence, it rounds up, to the nearest penny, in the family's favour. Always, with no threshold and no exception. The rounding costs fractions of a penny and the argument it prevents costs more to answer than to concede.
    25. PB-0218 Where a family has more than one make-up outstanding, the money settles the oldest. Always the oldest, not the one being discussed and not the one that suits: it is the one nearest to expiring, so it costs the child least, and a rule with no judgement in it cannot be applied differently to two families.
    26. PB-0224 Where no cover is found for a lesson in a school, the make-up is issued automatically and the family is told that the lesson did not happen and that a make-up is on their account. If they would rather have the money, the ladder decides the rest.
  • A school's capPer school, on the school's own page

    The most places a school will take.

    What changing it does not do. Lowering a cap below the children already enrolled removes nobody. It stops new applications and nothing else - a cap is a door, not a broom.

    Reaches: New applications at that school. · Set in: The school's configuration.

    The rules behind it · 26
    1. PB-0051 Every night, and again fourteen and three days before an expiry, count the suitable slots still bookable. Fewer than three and the window extends by four weeks on its own, with the capacity evidence kept as it stood.
    2. PB-0078 A cap stops new applications. It never removes a child. A cap below the number already enrolled means exactly one thing: no more.
    3. PB-0079 Capacity proposes; a person confirms. An instrument is never flipped to waiting-list-only by the system alone.
    4. PB-0080 Effective capacity is the cap less the headroom held back, and the public form reads the effective number, never the room count.
    5. PB-0081 Every cap carries the date it takes effect and the reason it was set, appears on the school's timeline, and goes to the school's account contact as a plain summary.
    6. PB-0083 Where an instrument is off-offer but a place exists, let it through and open the schedule amendment in the same action, so the exception has an end date.
    7. PB-0084 Room time a school gives us is its capacity. Availability is not sellable capacity, and room time does not create bookable places.
    8. PB-0121 What the person was looking at when they decided is stored with the decision: the figures as they stood, the document version, the capacity as it was. A reason without the evidence behind it is a note, and a note is not a defence.
    9. PB-0173 Open a schedule amendment whenever an off-offer place is let through.
    10. PB-0183 Coming back is decided by availability and nothing else. If the original spot is free and they pay, they have it. If it is not, they are offered a different spot, and failing that the waiting list, in that order.
    11. PB-0188 Where a school asks to split, the answer is one fully funded place rather than half each for two. One or the other.
    12. PB-0107 A placement that breaks a hard constraint is not a worse placement. It is not a placement, and there is no control that approves one anyway.
    13. PB-0108 A batch never moves a child who is already placed. It fills what is empty and proposes what is new.
    14. PB-0109 A proposal expires seven days after it is made. A stale proposal committed on day nine is worse than no proposal at all.
    15. PB-0110 What the scheduler weighs, out of 100: filling a half-empty group 40, fewer separate visits per teacher 25, keeping a group to one year 15, the same room each week 10, starting sooner 10, siblings together 10, the day the family asked for 5, an even load across teachers 5.
    16. PB-0111 A proposal commits without a person only when it clears 72 on the score and 8 on the gap to the next best, and is not a kind that always goes to a person.
    17. PB-0147 Practice note: released to the family as soon as the teacher writes it, not batched.
    18. PB-0175 A school that stops funding a place pays to the end of the term the place is in. The place then closes.
    19. PB-0176 A closing funded place does not convert to the family. Converting would put a family into a consumer contract they never signed, so the place closes and the family is offered a new one on their own terms if they want it.
    20. PB-0177 The family is told before the term ends, told what continuing would cost them, and offered the choice. Silence closes the place. Nobody is enrolled by not answering.
    21. PB-0179 There is no arrears ladder because there are no arrears. A term that has not been paid for is a term that was never scheduled: nothing is timetabled and no child is told they have a place in it.
    22. PB-0180 Once a term is scheduled it runs to the end of that term whatever happens to the invoice. No child is ever removed because a school has not paid. There is no command for it and there is not going to be one.
    23. PB-0182 A family whose place was removed in the ordinary way - the deposit covered the last lesson and the place closed - can come back. There is no penalty period and no going to the back of the queue.
    24. PB-0184 A teacher who leaves does not take pupils with them. It is a hard no. It is not a judgement call and it is not negotiated case by case.
    25. PB-0185 The same answer applies the other way round. A school that ends with us does not keep the teachers we placed there and run the service itself. It happens, and the answer is the same no.
    26. PB-0187 A place has one payer. A school funding half and a family funding the rest is not represented, not built and not agreed to.
  • Headroom held backPer school, subtracted from the cap before the form sees it

    Places kept out of the public form so a school is never sold its own last slot by a stranger.

    What changing it does not do. It does not reserve those places for anybody in particular. It only stops the form offering them.

    Reaches: What the public application form reads as capacity. · Set in: The school's configuration.

    The rules behind it · 11
    1. PB-0051 Every night, and again fourteen and three days before an expiry, count the suitable slots still bookable. Fewer than three and the window extends by four weeks on its own, with the capacity evidence kept as it stood.
    2. PB-0078 A cap stops new applications. It never removes a child. A cap below the number already enrolled means exactly one thing: no more.
    3. PB-0079 Capacity proposes; a person confirms. An instrument is never flipped to waiting-list-only by the system alone.
    4. PB-0080 Effective capacity is the cap less the headroom held back, and the public form reads the effective number, never the room count.
    5. PB-0081 Every cap carries the date it takes effect and the reason it was set, appears on the school's timeline, and goes to the school's account contact as a plain summary.
    6. PB-0083 Where an instrument is off-offer but a place exists, let it through and open the schedule amendment in the same action, so the exception has an end date.
    7. PB-0084 Room time a school gives us is its capacity. Availability is not sellable capacity, and room time does not create bookable places.
    8. PB-0121 What the person was looking at when they decided is stored with the decision: the figures as they stood, the document version, the capacity as it was. A reason without the evidence behind it is a note, and a note is not a defence.
    9. PB-0173 Open a schedule amendment whenever an off-offer place is let through.
    10. PB-0183 Coming back is decided by availability and nothing else. If the original spot is free and they pay, they have it. If it is not, they are offered a different spot, and failing that the waiting list, in that order.
    11. PB-0188 Where a school asks to split, the answer is one fully funded place rather than half each for two. One or the other.
  • When a reminder goesPer message kind, in the message policy

    How far ahead a family or school is told something is due.

    What changing it does not do. It does not change a notice period. A notice starts when it is sent, and sending a reminder earlier does not start it earlier.

    Reaches: Reminders sent after the change. · Set in: The message policy.

    The rules behind it · 13
    1. PB-0006 A new rule enters the playbook only when the owner rules on a real case. Nobody writes policy in the abstract. The case that produced it is kept against it as the reason.
    2. PB-0007 A rule starts provisional: ruled once, on one real case. It is applied from then on, and watched.
    3. PB-0012 Every rule keeps the case that produced it, the date it was ruled, how many times it has been applied and what happened each time.
    4. PB-0014 Nothing is ever unlearned quietly. A rule that is replaced keeps its history, and the case that broke it is kept against the one that replaced it.
    5. PB-0038 While a removal case is open the place is still theirs: no slot released, no entitlement cancelled, no payment state moved. It waits for a person and will wait for ever.
    6. PB-0039 A removal case has to carry every attempt to collect and what came back, every notice sent and whether it arrived, and where the lessons happen and who else is affected.
    7. PB-0117 Nothing reaches a school or a parent before the safeguarding lead has read it.
    8. PB-0118 A case will not close while an action, a message or a piece of evidence is still missing. Closing quietly is how a family finds out weeks later that nobody wrote.
    9. PB-0119 A case with no action against it was never worked - it was only read.
    10. PB-0120 A record is never edited or removed. A mistake is a new record that points at the old one, and both stay.
    11. PB-0128 Absence is a welfare signal and sometimes a safeguarding one. It is never a sales signal.
    12. PB-0156 Signature outstanding: at seven days and at fourteen, then it becomes a case rather than a reminder.
    13. PB-0174 Nothing that moves money out, changes a rate, ends a place, or touches safeguarding. One exception, and only one: a proven duplicate collection is refunded under PB-0166 without asking anybody, because going back to a family for permission to return money we took twice is worse than returning it.

  • The arrears ladderFixed steps, fixed days

    What happens, and when, after a collection fails.

    What changing it does not do. It never runs on a collection that failed because we could not ask. A provider we could not reach is not a family who did not pay.

    Reaches: Every failed collection. · Set in: Nowhere. It is a rule.

    The rules behind it · 17
    1. PB-0033 A failed draw is per child, not per family. It removes only the affected child; siblings carry on and their deposits are their own.
    2. PB-0034 A cancelled or changed instruction is a failure, whatever the intention, and runs the same ladder as a refusal.
    3. PB-0035 Arrears past what the deposit covers - that is one month - and the child comes off the diary until it clears.
    4. PB-0036 A school-funded place creates no parent mandate, no deposit and no billing relationship with the family. It can never appear on failed payments or proposed removals.
    5. PB-0038 While a removal case is open the place is still theirs: no slot released, no entitlement cancelled, no payment state moved. It waits for a person and will wait for ever.
    6. PB-0039 A removal case has to carry every attempt to collect and what came back, every notice sent and whether it arrived, and where the lessons happen and who else is affected.
    7. PB-0041 Where a family with a clean two-year history misses because of illness, hold the removal and spread the arrears rather than release the place.
    8. PB-0077 The school ledger inherits nothing from the parent one. No deposit, no mandate, no dunning, no arrears disclosure - not disabled, not zero-valued: absent.
    9. PB-0127 They are never told the school's rate, balance, arrears or that it is late, and never anything implying they might have to pay instead.
    10. PB-0182 A family whose place was removed in the ordinary way - the deposit covered the last lesson and the place closed - can come back. There is no penalty period and no going to the back of the queue.
    11. PB-0025 Money is allocated the day it lands, in this order: deposits, teacher pay, refunds reserve, software and overheads, free cash. Free cash is nothing until the four above are full.
    12. PB-0030 A deposit is one month at the current plan price, standing behind each place, and it follows the price.
    13. PB-0031 No monthly collection runs until the deposit settles and a first lesson is on the diary.
    14. PB-0032 A deposit may only be applied to an invoice that was contractually due, or to the final invoice. Every application names the invoice it went to.
    15. PB-0037 When a place ends the last month is charged in full, the deposit goes against the final invoice, and any balance is returned within fourteen days of the final amount being established.
    16. PB-0044 Only two things produce a refund: the deposit balance when a place ends, and a discretionary decision that has been through approval.
    17. PB-0151 Deposit outstanding: at seven days, then at fourteen, with what it is holding up.

What nothing may ever do 6

Not settings that are switched off. Commands that do not exist, so there is nothing to switch on.

  1. PB-0015 Create, edit or delete an attendance record.
  2. PB-0016 Create, edit or delete a practice note.
  3. PB-0017 Edit a signature, or the text it was signed against.
  4. PB-0018 Convert a place from one payer to the other.
  5. PB-0019 Put an expiry on a make-up we caused.
  6. PB-0020 Remove a child because a school has not paid.
Why this list is twelve long and not two hundred

Almost everything that looks like a setting in a system like this is a rule wearing a switch. A rule is the same for everybody and changes by being ruled on, with the case that caused it on the record. A setting is a number somebody may choose, within limits, with a consequence.

Treating the first as the second is how a business ends up unable to say what it does, because what it does depends on what somebody set last Tuesday and nobody wrote down why. The rules are in the playbook.
